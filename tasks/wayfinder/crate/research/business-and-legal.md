# Which business models and legal basics apply

Access date for all sources: 2026-11-22. Ticket 09. Not legal advice: this file states what the primary texts say and marks where a lawyer or a gestor (tax and Social Security adviser) is needed.

Reading conventions. **Verified** means I opened the text myself. "Via mirror" means I read it on a copy of the official text: EUR-Lex returned an empty bot-challenge page (HTTP 202) to every request, so the CJEU rulings were read on ipcuria.eu and the EU regulations in Spanish on the BOE's copy of the Official Journal. "Search summary" means only a search tool's summary of the page was available. Anything not opened is marked **not verified**. Inferences are labelled (inference). Facts about the platforms' own terms come from the earlier studies in this folder and are cited by file name.

## Answer

- **Linking to official streaming pages is lawful in EU law, and so is embedding the platforms' own players; the real exposure is the platforms' contract terms, not copyright.** Svensson (C-466/12) held that clickable links to works freely available with the rightholder's consent are not a "communication to the public"; BestWater (C-348/13) said the same for framing; VG Bild-Kunst (C-392/19) made an exception only where the rightholder applied effective technical measures against framing. A for-profit linker to an unauthorised upload is presumed to know it (GS Media, C-160/15), so link to official services, not to random uploads. Via mirror, section "Linking, embedding and clips".
- **Audio clips: the safest default is to host none.** Every route is ranked in the clip table below. Link-out (no audio) is risk-free and free. Playing the platform's own embed, started by the listener, is the next safest and free. Playing a platform's raw preview file in your own player breaches terms (Spotify, Apple, Deezer). Self-hosting even a few seconds needs licences from the recording's producer (and the publisher and performers), and Spanish quotation law does not cover it.
- **There is no free, lawful route to a blind "guess the song" clip (inference).** Every free embed shows the title and artwork, which gives the answer away, and the free raw-preview routes are contract breaches. A blind clip needs either a written approval (Spotify, free to ask, uncertain), or a licensed clip provider such as 7digital (price not published: a paid gate), or labels' licences (not realistic for one person).
- **Business models ranked by legal safety, then cost (safest first):** free; donations; host pays or premium for your own features; affiliate links (Apple, Amazon, ticketing); sponsorship of rounds; display ads. Ads are last because Apple's agreement names advertising, Spotify and YouTube restrict ads around their players, and ad networks bring cookie-consent work. Table below.
- **A platform's developer terms bind only what you use.** A product that only sends users to public URLs touches none of Spotify's, Apple's, Deezer's or TIDAL's developer agreements (inference: you never accept them). Embeds bring in Spotify's Widget Terms (games need written approval) and YouTube's API terms. Using MusicKit brings in Apple's DPLA 3.3.6(D) ban on monetising Apple Music access.
- **Affiliate money exists but is small and partly unverified.** Apple Services Performance Partners is the only sanctioned route Apple itself promotes (amount unpublished). Amazon Associates Spain shows a 3 EUR bounty per Amazon Music Unlimited trial sign-up (page footer 2025). Ticketmaster Spain via Impact is reported at about 1 percent (secondary). No Spotify or YouTube Premium affiliate programme was found.
- **From the first user you are a data controller under GDPR.** No exemption for a one-person site. In Spain consent given by a minor is valid from age 14 (LOPDGDD art. 7); a record of processing is still required in practice (GDPR art. 30(5)); no DPO is needed; breaches go to the AEPD within 72 hours. Verified.
- **Newsletters need prior consent; cookies need consent before they are set.** LSSI art. 21 bans unsolicited commercial email and allows a narrow existing-customer exception; art. 22.2 requires informed consent for non-essential cookies or device storage. Fines run up to 30,000 EUR (minor) and 30,001 to 150,000 EUR (serious). Verified in the BOE text. Platform embeds set cookies, so load them on click (inference).
- **The DSA applies to you as a hosting service, with the heavy parts waived for micro and small firms.** You need a contact point, terms that explain moderation, and a notice-and-action form. The online-platform section (complaints, trusted flaggers, ad transparency, minors) and the transparency reports do not apply to micro or small enterprises. Spain's national enforcement law was still not in force on the CNMC's page (undated). Verified via mirror.
- **As an individual in Spain, the first regular income triggers registration, and no income threshold is a safe harbour.** Social Security requires registration for habitual, personal, lucrative activity (LGSS art. 305). The Supreme Court (STS 941/2025) says income below the minimum wage (17,094 EUR a year in 2026) is not in general an excluding factor. Lowest 2026 contribution base is 653.59 EUR a month, about 200 EUR in contributions (my arithmetic), with a reduced "tarifa plana" whose 2026 amount I could not confirm.

Two points hold for every row. First, none of these terms was tested against a real approval request; I found no platform page that gives a safe-harbour list for a game. Second, once a model brings income, the individual must register as self-employed (section "Running it as an individual in Spain").

**Decision-relevant reading (inference).** The cheapest compliant start is rank 1, with rank 3 for host-pays added later because it sells your own features and not platform access. Rank 4 can be added as outbound links without any platform API. Ranks 5 and 6 are where the platform clauses bite hardest.

## Linking, embedding and clips

### What the Court of Justice held (via mirror, ipcuria.eu)

| Case | Date, ECLI | Holding, in the Court's words | Applied to this product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Svensson, C-466/12 | 13 Feb 2014, ECLI:EU:C:2014:76 | Provision "on a website of clickable links to works freely available on another website" "does not constitute an 'act of communication to the public'". Authorisation is needed where a link circumvents access restrictions (para 31). | A link from a league page to an `open.spotify.com`, `music.apple.com` or `youtube.com` page of a song needs no licence: the page is the rightholder's own authorised communication. A link does not bypass a login or subscription, it only leads to it. |
| BestWater, C-348/13 (order) | 21 Oct 2014, ECLI:EU:C:2014:2315 | Inserting a freely available work into another site by framing "cannot [be] classified as 'communication to the public'" since it reaches no new public and uses no different technical method. | Embedding the platform's own iframe player is on the same line. |
| GS Media, C-160/15 | 8 Sep 2016, ECLI:EU:C:2016:644 | For links to works posted without consent, one must ask whether the links are provided without financial gain by someone who did not know the publication was illegal, or for profit, "a situation in which that knowledge must be presumed" (rebuttable, para 51). | If the game earns money (ads, subscription, affiliate), links to unauthorised uploads (a pirate YouTube re-upload) could be your infringement. Prefer official artist, label and "Topic" uploads, and remove on notice. The presumption is of knowledge, not of liability: you may rebut it. |
| Renckhoff, C-161/17 | 7 Aug 2018, ECLI:EU:C:2018:634 | Re-posting on another website a photo previously posted with consent is a communication to the public; the link case law does not apply to copying. | Do not copy cover art or audio to your own server. Hotlink or embed what the platform serves (inference). |
| VG Bild-Kunst, C-392/19 | 9 Mar 2021, ECLI:EU:C:2021:181 | Embedding by framing "where that embedding circumvents measures adopted or imposed by that copyright holder to provide protection from framing" is a communication to the public. The holder "cannot be allowed to limit his or her consent by means other than effective technological measures" (para 46). | Using the platform-supplied embed code does not circumvent any measure. A contractual ban in the platform's terms (such as Spotify's games clause) is not an "effective technological measure", so it probably does not turn framing into copyright infringement, but it can still cost you the embed or the account (inference; Spotify may not be the rightholder, so a lawyer should confirm). |

Spanish rules that sit beside this, verified in the BOE text of the Ley 34/2002 (LSSI): art. 17 exempts a provider who links to content from liability unless it has "conocimiento efectivo" of illegality (for instance a court or competent body has declared it and the provider knows) and fails to remove the link diligently. Art. 16 does the same for hosting. These articles were suppressed by Real Decreto-ley 9/2024 and restored when Congress repealed that decree (BOE consolidated text, last update 23/01/2025). For the EU-wide hosting exemption see DSA art. 6 (via mirror): no liability if you lack actual knowledge or, once aware, act promptly to remove.

### Stories, newsletters and cover art

Shareable "stories" and newsletters will carry song titles and cover art. Song titles are facts; cover art is copyrighted. Apple's rules say cover art and metadata "may only be used in connection with music playback or playlists ... and should not be used in any marketing or advertising without getting specific authorization from rights-holders" (apple-music-access.md, guideline 4.5.2(ii)), and Spotify requires cover art to link back and not be cropped or overlaid (spotify-access.md). Renckhoff says copying artwork to your own server is a separate act. Safest default: text-only story cards (inference); a lawyer should confirm before using artwork in generated images.

## Data protection, minors, cookies, email

All GDPR points verified via mirror (BOE copy of Regulation (EU) 2016/679) unless marked.

- **Household exemption does not cover you.** Art. 2(2)(c) excludes processing "por una persona física en el ejercicio de actividades exclusivamente personales o domésticas"; a public service run for users is not that (inference).
- **Roles and basis.** You are the controller of account data, votes and submissions. Likely bases: contract for running the game (art. 6(1)(b)), consent for newsletters and non-essential cookies, legitimate interest for security (inference). Votes are personal data when tied to an account even if shown anonymously to other players (inference).
- **Information duty.** A privacy notice at collection (art. 13). Processors you use (hosting, email service, payment provider) need contracts (art. 28). Transfers outside the EEA need a basis (chapter V; art. 44 onward).
- **Records, DPO, breaches.** Art. 30(5) exempts organisations under 250 staff unless the processing is not occasional, risky, or includes special categories: running a service is not occasional, so keep a short record. A DPO is required only for public bodies, large-scale systematic monitoring, or large-scale special categories (art. 37(1)); not you. Breaches notified to the AEPD "sin dilación indebida" and within 72 hours where possible (art. 33). Fines: up to 20 million EUR or 4 percent of turnover at the upper tier (art. 83(5)); in practice small controllers get warnings first (AEPD practice: not verified).
- **Minors.** GDPR art. 8 sets 16 and lets states go down to 13. Spain chose 14: "El tratamiento de los datos personales de un menor de edad únicamente podrá fundarse en su consentimiento cuando sea mayor de catorce años" (LOPDGDD art. 7(1); under 14 needs the holder of parental authority, art. 7(2)). Art. 8 applies to consent as the basis; for contract as the basis, a minor's capacity to contract is a separate civil-law question (inference). Simplest route: state a minimum age of 14 in the terms, collect the minimum data, and ask a lawyer before targeting under-14s. A government draft to raise the age is reported but I did not open it (not verified).
- **Newsletters and commercial email.** LSSI art. 21(1): "Queda prohibido el envío de comunicaciones publicitarias o promocionales por correo electrónico ... que previamente no hubieran sido solicitadas o expresamente autorizadas". Art. 21(2) lifts this only where there is a prior contractual relationship, the contact data were obtained lawfully, and the message is about "productos o servicios de su propia empresa que sean similares", with a simple free opt-out at collection and in every message (the soft opt-in). Art. 22(1): revocation by simple notice. Mass or persistent breaches are serious infringements (art. 38(3)(c)); fines 30,001 to 150,000 EUR for serious, up to 30,000 EUR for minor (art. 39). Practical reading (inference): use an explicit opt-in checkbox; round reminders and results that the user asked for are service messages, but adding affiliate links or promotions makes the message promotional. Identify commercial messages as such (art. 20).
- **Cookies.** LSSI art. 22(2): non-essential storage on the user's device only "después de que se les haya facilitado información clara y completa" and with consent; strictly necessary storage is exempt. The AEPD cookie guide (updated May 2024) says the first layer needs an accept button and an equal-level reject button, and analytics cookies are not exempt in general; its January 2024 guide on audience-measurement tools says some first-party statistical cookies can be exempt under conditions (both via search summary; PDFs not readable) ([guide](https://www.aepd.es/guias/guia-cookies.pdf), [analytics guide](https://www.aepd.es/guias/guia-cookies-analiticas-externas.pdf)). Embedded players from Spotify, YouTube and Apple store or read data on the device, and Spotify's and YouTube's terms require a cookie and privacy disclosure (spotify-access.md, youtube-access.md). Inference: load embeds only after the user clicks, use cookieless or first-party analytics, and no ad network, so no banner is needed beyond the click.
- **Site information.** LSSI art. 10(1)(a) and (e): the site must show your name, domicile (or address of a Spanish establishment), email and tax ID. As an individual that is your home address unless you use an office address (inference).
- **YouTube-specific.** If YouTube is used, the privacy policy must name YouTube API services and link Google's policy (youtube-access.md).

## DSA and consumer law

### Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065), via mirror

- **Which category.** The game is an intermediary: a "servicio de alojamiento de datos" the moment it stores what users give it (usernames, submissions, comments) (art. 3(g)). It is an "online platform" only if it also disseminates that information "al público" (art. 3(i)), meaning to a "número potencialmente ilimitado de terceros" (art. 3(k)). Recital 14 says that where access needs admission to a group, the information counts as disseminated to the public only if people are admitted automatically "sin que un ser humano decida o escoja a quién se concede el acceso". A closed friends league run by a host is outside; a public daily game, public leaderboard or public comments is inside (inference).
- **Duties for all intermediaries.** A single contact point for authorities and one for users, by electronic means, not only automated tools (arts. 11 and 12); terms that describe moderation policies and tools (art. 14); art. 13 (legal representative) applies only to providers with no EU establishment, not to you.
- **Duties for hosting services.** A notice-and-action mechanism that is "de fácil acceso y manejo", electronic, asking for the reason, exact URL, name and email, and a good-faith statement (art. 16); acknowledge receipt and tell the notifier your decision (art. 16(4)-(5)); give a statement of reasons to the user whose content you restrict (art. 17); report suspected crimes threatening life or safety (art. 18). No liability without actual knowledge, and prompt removal once aware (art. 6).
- **What micro and small firms are spared.** Section 3 (online-platform duties, arts. 19 to 28: internal complaints, out-of-court dispute settlement, trusted flaggers, ad transparency, protection of minors) "no se aplicará a los prestadores de plataformas en línea que sean microempresas o pequeñas empresas" (art. 19(1)), except art. 24(3): you must give the Commission or the coordinator, on request, your average monthly active EU recipients. Transparency reports (art. 15(1)) do not apply to micro or small enterprises (art. 15(2)). The definition is Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC (under 50 staff and turnover or balance sheet up to 10 million EUR for small; not opened). The exemption lapses 12 months after you stop qualifying (art. 19(1)).
- **Spain.** The CNMC was designated Digital Services Coordinator in January 2024, but its page says the law that gives it all powers "has not yet" been approved (undated) ([CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/en/sectores-que-regulamos/servicios-digitales-dsa/reglamento)). A bill went to Congress on 29 July 2025 (search summary); I could not confirm its present stage. So the regulation applies directly, but Spanish penalties for it were not in force at the time of the CNMC page.

### Consumer law, if there are paid features (verified in the BOE text of the TRLGDCU, RDLeg 1/2007)

- **Withdrawal.** The consumer has "un plazo mínimo de catorce días naturales" to withdraw from a distance contract (art. 71(1)); if you fail to give the withdrawal information, the period extends by up to 12 months (art. 71(3)).
- **Digital content exception.** Art. 103(m): no withdrawal for digital content not on a physical medium when performance has begun and, for paid content, the consumer (1) gave prior consent to start during the withdrawal period, (2) acknowledged loss of the right, and (3) you provided the confirmation required by arts. 98(7) or 99(2). Art. 103(a): for services, the right is lost only once the service has been fully performed, with the same consent and knowledge. A recurring subscription to a service is not fully performed at sign-up, so the 14 days probably apply (inference; ask a lawyer).
- **Withdrawal button.** Directive (EU) 2023/2673 adds a withdrawal function for online distance contracts, applicable from 19 June 2026 (BOE annotation of the Directive, via mirror). Spain's transposition: not verified.
- **Prices and info.** LSSI art. 10(1)(f): clear price information, stating whether taxes are included. LSSI art. 20: commercial communications and promotional offers must be identifiable and conditions easily accessible.
- **Prizes.** Ley 13/2011 (gambling) covers "rifas y concursos, en los que la participación se realiza mediante una contraprestación económica" (art. 2(1)(b)) and any activity where money or items of economic value are staked on uncertain results even if skill predominates (art. 3(a)). Do not combine paid entry with prizes without legal advice; free, prize-less leagues are not the target of that law (inference).

## Running it as an individual in Spain

- **Hacienda.** An individual who starts an economic activity files the censo declaration (modelo 036) before starting; the older simplified modelo 037 was abolished by Orden HAC/1526/2024 (AEAT summary via search tool; I opened [the AEAT registration page](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/censos-nif-domicilio-fiscal/tramites-censales-relacionados-empresarios-profesionales-retenedores/darme-alta-censo-empresarios-profesionales-retenedores.html), which did not state when an activity begins or any income threshold). I found no income threshold on the Hacienda side.
- **Seguridad Social, who must register.** LGSS art. 305(1) covers persons over 18 "que realicen de forma habitual, personal, directa, por cuenta propia ... una actividad económica o profesional a título lucrativo". **Habitualidad** has no statutory euro threshold in the text I read. The Supreme Court, STS 941/2025 of 10 July 2025, held that comparing income with the minimum wage (SMI) is an indicator, and that income below it is "no es, con carácter general, un elemento excluyente de la habitualidad" ([BOE legal journal summary](https://www.boe.es/biblioteca_juridica/anuarios_derecho/articulo.php?id=ANU-L-2025-00000003060)). The 2026 SMI is 1,221 EUR a month ([Real Decreto 126/2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2026-3815)), 17,094 EUR a year over 14 payments (my arithmetic). So there is neither a safe zone below nor a duty above that figure by itself; the indicator is income, regularity and aim of profit (inference). Ask the Tesorería General (TGSS) or a gestor before the first euro of regular income.
- **Cost.** Order PJC/297/2026 sets 2026 bases by estimated net yield. For yield up to 670 EUR a month the base runs from 653.59 to 718.94 EUR. Rates: 28.30 percent common contingencies, 1.30 percent occupational, 0.90 percent intergenerational equity mechanism ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2026-7296)). At the minimum base that is about 199 EUR a month; the cessation-of-activity and training percentages (art. 37 of the Order) add roughly 1 point (my arithmetic, not checked). Reduced rate: Real Decreto-ley 13/2022 transitional provision 5 fixed 80 EUR a month for 2023 to 2025, and "a partir del año 2026" the budget law sets it ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-12482)). Seguridad Social pages I saw still describe 80 EUR for 12 months (search summary); the 2026 amount is **not verified**.
- **Invoicing.** RD 1619/2012 art. 2(1): businesspeople "están obligados a expedir factura" for supplies "en el desarrollo de su actividad"; art. 2(2)(a): always when the recipient is a business or asks for it for tax purposes (verified, BOE). Affiliate commissions are services to a business (Apple, Amazon, an ad network), so you invoice them; whether VAT applies depends on where they are established (inference; gestor).
- **VAT on sales to consumers.** Electronic services to consumers in other EU states are taxed where the consumer is, except while your cross-border B2C sales across the EU stay at or below 10,000 EUR a year (excluding VAT), when your Spanish VAT applies; above that you charge each state's VAT and declare through the one-stop shop (AEAT pages via search tool: [guide](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/static_files/Sede/Tema/IVA/Comercio_electr/Guia_ventanilla_unica.pdf)). Payment platforms that act as merchant of record would change this (not researched).
- **E-invoicing software (Verifactu).** Per secondary sources, Real Decreto-ley 15/2025 moved the obligation to 1 January 2027 for corporate-tax payers and 1 July 2027 for others, and the ministry has announced a further delay to 2028 that needs a new BOE norm; none opened, **not verified**.
- **Personal liability.** As an individual (autónomo) you answer with personal assets for claims (general rule, not verified in this study). A label's copyright claim or a consumer claim would reach you directly; this is the strongest practical reason to keep audio off the product and to consider a limited company before revenue grows (inference).
- **Other Spanish rules not covered.** Regional rules on consumer complaint forms, intellectual-property registration of the product, trade marks, and the minors-in-digital-environments law: not researched.
